Advance Three Spaces
Find your tribe.
A vision for connection in Pasco County  ·  Patrick Quinlan, Executive Director

My father spent his last decades
watching TV.

He let his friendships fall away until there was almost no one left — my mother, the television, and a drink. His whole world shrank to one room. After he passed, I found photos of him playing pinochle, showing off his new car to friends, newspaper clippings about being debate club captain, and I realized I had never known that version of him.

At 42, I was diagnosed
as autistic.

Suddenly my whole life made sense — why I'd only had two friends growing up. Why I always stood at the periphery of every party. Why I'd struggled with office banter. Why, at the time, I knew lots of people but still felt lonely.

I realized I was on the same path.

I could see exactly how I could end up just like my dad. But learning there was a cause behind my social struggles also gave me hope that there were things I could do to change my situation.

I started A3S for me.

I decided that I needed to be purposeful in making friends, so I invited over all the husbands I knew to play board games. We played, we talked, and in fact, the topics ran pretty deep.

One of them told me afterward

“We actualy talked about
something real.”

I did not expect what I heard the next day. One of the guys said he'd never had a real conversation with friends like that before. The next game night, it happened again: we started the night playing games, and ended talking about infertility. I could see the need to talk, to connect, and to be heard. So I did a little digging and it didn't take long for me to learn that we were far from the only ones seeking connection.

Loneliness is an epidemic.

According to the Surgeon General, about half of us report feeling lonely, with young adults
reporting the highest rates.

It's our neighbors.

To grow, we need a home.

Today that community meets in borrowed rooms — a coffee shop here, a library there, packed up at the end of every night. Imagine what it becomes with a place of its own.

A park you can walk into.
Rain or shine.

Step off a hot parking lot into a field of green. Astroturf. Potted trees. Tables everywhere. Free to everyone; a permanent, public place built to make connection easy.

The Commons  ·  The Market  ·  The Stage  ·  The Library
The Commons

Green space that invites you to linger.

The heart of The Park is the Commons, an open, turf-covered gathering floor. Pull up a chair at a game already going, spread out with your kids, or simply sit and be around other people. It's the town square we stopped building.

The Market

Local makers who
teach you their craft.

Surrounding the Commons is a ring of small vendor stalls--coffee, cottage foods, local produce--run by neighbors, not chains. Vendors don't just sell; each one teaches a class and mentors newcomers. It's the living economy that gives the room its energy and gives people a reason to keep coming back.

The Stage

A place to be seen.

Anchoring the far end is the Stage, hosting community theatre, movie nights, a local band, a kid's first performance. Somewhere the whole room can gather around one shared moment — and where someone who has never been in the spotlight finally gets to shine.

The Game Library

Hundreds of games, always
free to play.

And of course, the thing that started it all — a wall of games anyone can pull down and play, with someone always ready to teach the rules. The lowest-pressure way in the world to sit down across from a stranger and, an hour later, get up as friends.

A permanent third space that builds true community.
